## What "scientific" actually means

A basic calculator does arithmetic. A scientific calculator adds the functions that school and work actually demand: trigonometry (sin, cos, tan and their inverses), logarithms (log base 10 and natural log), exponents and roots, factorials and combinations, constants (π, e), and angle modes (degrees vs radians — the source of half of all trig errors).

## The 10 functions students use most

In practice, most classwork comes down to ten operations: sine/cosine/tangent, inverse trig, log₁₀, natural log, xʸ (powers), √ and nth roots, factorials, π and e, parentheses for order of operations, and the Ans key (reuse the last result instead of retyping and rounding). Master these ten and you've covered 95% of high-school and early-college needs.

## Degrees vs radians: the classic trap

If sin(30) gives you -0.988 instead of 0.5, you're in radian mode. Geometry and most high-school problems use degrees; calculus and physics often use radians. Check the mode indicator before you compute — it takes one second and saves the whole problem.

## Beyond the basics: matrices and graphing

Linear algebra means matrices — determinants, inverses, multiplication. A good online scientific calculator handles 2×2 and 3×3 matrices without you doing cofactor expansions by hand. And graphing turns abstract functions into pictures: plot y = x² - 4 and the roots stare back at you.
